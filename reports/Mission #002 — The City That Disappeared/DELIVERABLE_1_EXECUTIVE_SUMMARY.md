# DELIVERABLE 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
## The Hydro-Climatic Squeeze, Infrastructure Lock-In, and the Gradual Death of Greater Angkor

### 1.1 The Forensic Reality: From Romantic Myth to Systems Thermodynamics
For over a century, western popular consciousness framed Angkor as a mystical, sudden disappearance—a golden civilization swallowed instantaneously by the Cambodian jungle, or snuffed out overnight in 1431 CE by a catastrophic Siamese (Ayutthaya) invasion. 

Interdisciplinary scientific breakthroughs over the last fifteen years—specifically **Airborne Laser Scanning (LiDAR) by the Khmer Archaeology LiDAR Consortium (KALC)**, **759-year dendrochronological paleoclimate reconstructions from Vietnamese cypress (*Fokienia hodginsii*) by Buckley et al. (Columbia Lamont-Doherty)**, and **geoarchaeological sediment trenching by Dan Penny, Roland Fletcher, and Christophe Pottier (The Greater Angkor Project)**—have completely shattered the romantic catastrophic narrative.

Angkor was not a compact, walled ceremonial city that vanished overnight. It was **the most extensive low-density urban settlement complex in the pre-industrial world**, sprawling over **1,000 square kilometers** and supporting an estimated population of **750,000 to 900,000 inhabitants**. Its survival was underpinned by an unprecedented, continent-scale artificial hydraulic network consisting of massive reservoirs (*barays*), thousands of kilometers of interconnected canals, dikes, spillways, and diverted rivers (Siem Reap, Puok, and Roluos Rivers).

### 1.2 The Core Explanatory Thesis: Inelastic Infrastructural Lock-In under Climatic Volatility
The disappearance of Angkor represents a classic case of **Thermodynamic and Infrastructural Sclerosis under Exogenous Climate Whiplash**:
1. **The Construction of Systemic Hyper-Inelasticity:** For four centuries (9th to 13th centuries CE), the Khmer god-kings (*Devaraja*) progressively expanded Angkor's hydraulic infrastructure to mitigate the erratic variability of the Southeast Asian Summer Monsoon. However, each expansion bound the city into an increasingly rigid, path-dependent topology. Canals were engineered with fixed slopes, gravity feeds, and earthen embankments designed for predictable seasonal discharge.
2. **The 14th–15th Century Climatic Volatility Shock:** The onset of the Little Ice Age destabilized the Pacific decadal oscillation and the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ). Tree-ring data documents two multi-decade megadroughts:
   - **Drought 1 (c. 1345–1374 CE):** The most severe drought in Southeast Asia in over a millennium.
   - **Drought 2 (c. 1401–1425 CE):** A prolonged period of extreme aridity.
3. **The Destructive Monsoon Whiplash:** Crucially, these megadroughts were punctuated not by normal rains, but by **unprecedented, hyper-intense mega-monsoon flood years** (such as the extreme La Niña deluges of the 1370s and 1420s).
4. **The Cascading Hydro-Engineering Failure:** 
   - During droughts, canals dried, dropped velocity, and accumulated catastrophic sedimentation layers.
   - When the mega-monsoons hit, the silted, rigid network could not absorb the hyper-volume of water. Rivers breached earthen embankments, bypassing flow controls.
   - Massive erosion destroyed the critical masonry masonry spillways (such as the Siem Reap river bypass at **Siem Reap Gate and Spean Thma**), incising riverbeds by up to **5 to 8 meters** below the canal intakes.
   - Once the rivers downcut into deep canyons, the entire gravity-fed reservoir network could no longer be refilled. The agricultural heartland lost its dry-season irrigation, collapsing wet-rice yields.
5. **The Gradual Dematerialization of the State:** Faced with systemic agricultural collapse, the population did not starve en masse; they migrated. Society dematerialized, abandoning the costly monument-building corvée economy and shifting south to the maritime trading confluence of Phnom Penh and Lovek along the Mekong River delta. The Ayutthaya military raid of 1431 was the coup de grâce on a dying, hollowed-out administrative husk, not the cause of its demise.
