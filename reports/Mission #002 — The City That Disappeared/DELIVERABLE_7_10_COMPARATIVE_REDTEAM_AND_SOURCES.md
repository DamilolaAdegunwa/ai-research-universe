# DELIVERABLE 8: RED-TEAM ADVERSARIAL AUDIT & CONTRADICTION REGISTER
## Subjecting the Climate-Hydraulic Collapse Thesis to Falsification Testing

A rigorous scientific inquiry must deliberately cross-examine the leading explanatory framework against competing hypotheses and evidentiary gaps.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                THE RED-TEAM ADVERSARIAL AUDIT                                      |
|                                                                                                    |
|  Counter-Thesis 1: "The Collapse was purely military (Siamese conquest of 1431)."                 |
|  Evidence Counter: LiDAR and radiocarbon dates prove elite depopulation began decades before 1431. |

|  Evidence Counter: Zero osteological or biomolecular evidence of Y. pestis in Southeast Asia.     |
|                                                                                                    |
|  Counter-Thesis 3: "Angkor was not abandoned; it was merely a religious transition."              |
|  Evidence Counter: Geoarchaeological strata prove physical destruction of trunk canals and dikes.  |

```

### 8.1 Detailed Falsification Testing

#### 1. The Pandemics Hypothesis (The Black Death)
* **The Counter-Argument:** The mid-14th century (1346–1353) witnessed the catastrophic Eurasian spread of the Black Death (*Yersinia pestis*). Some historians hypothesize that plague travelled down maritime trade routes from southern China into Southeast Asia, wiping out the urban workforce of Angkor and causing the canal maintenance collapse.
* **The Forensic Refutation:** To date, extensive skeletal excavations in Cambodia and Thailand have yielded **zero genomic or ancient DNA (aDNA) signatures of *Yersinia pestis***. Tropical flea vectors (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) behave differently under monsoon humidity, and Southeast Asian mortality patterns during this epoch do not display the sharp, catastrophic demographic cliff characteristic of European and Middle Eastern plague outbreaks.

#### 2. The Internal Civil War & Succession Crisis Argument
* **The Counter-Argument:** Epigraphic records from the 13th and 14th centuries indicate bitter factional fighting between Shaivite royalists and Buddhist reformers, including the violent defacement of thousands of Buddhist carvings at the Bayon temple during the Hindu reaction of Jayavarman VIII (c. 1243–1295).
* **Synthesis:** Political factionalism certainly degraded state coordination capacity. However, civil conflict alone cannot explain why an entire 1,000 km² agricultural plain was permanently abandoned. Political turmoil acted as a **vulnerability amplifier**, preventing the state from mobilizing the labor necessary to repair catastrophic hydrological damage caused by exogenous climatic whiplash.

# DELIVERABLE 9: STRATEGIC INVARIANTS & LESSONS FOR 21ST-CENTURY INFRASTRUCTURE
## What Modern Civil Engineers, City Planners, and Technologists Must Learn from Angkor

The rise and fall of Greater Angkor provides profound, actionable lessons for modern urbanized societies navigating 21st-century climate disruption:

1. **The Trap of Inelastic Infrastructural Lock-In:**  
   The larger, more integrated, and more rigid an infrastructure network becomes, the less adaptable it is to non-linear shocks. Angkor engineered its canals for narrow, predictable historical precipitation bands. When the climate regime shifted to extreme bimodal variance (megadroughts punctuated by super-storms), the very infrastructure built to protect the city became the instrument of its destruction. *Modern analogue: Concrete storm-drainage basins and channelized rivers in Houston, Jakarta, and Valencia that transform into catastrophic torrents under 500-year rain events.*
2. **Maintenance Costs Grow Faster Than System Capacity:**  
   Every kilometer of canal and dike added to Angkor created a compounding liability of annual sediment clearing, erosion reinforcement, and sluice gate repair. When societal ideological cohesion eroded, the system entered an irreversible death spiral of maintenance debt. *Modern analogue: The trillions of dollars in deferred maintenance across aging Western highway bridges, water mains, and electrical transmission grids.*
3. **Decentralized Modularity Trumps Monolithic Integration:**  
   Angkor's downfall was triggered because upstream breaches in the northern collector canals cascaded downstream through the entire interconnected grid. Autonomous, decentralized communal water harvesting units survived long after the imperial trunk canals incised and died.
4. **Geography Always Reclaims Economic Gravity:**  
   When climate disruption destroyed the internal agricultural surplus of the interior plain, economic survival demanded moving to the coast. Societies that refuse to reallocate capital away from stranded interior assets toward adaptive maritime and commercial hubs inevitably decay.

# DELIVERABLE 10: PRIMARY EPIGRAPHIC, DIPLOMATIC & SCIENTIFIC BIBLIOGRAPHY

### 1. Primary Textual & Diplomatic Sources
1. **Zhou Daguan (周达观)** (1297 CE) — *Zhenla Fengtuji* (*The Customs of Cambodia / Record of A Country in the Wind*). Translated by Peter Harris (Silkworm Books, 2007). The only surviving eyewitness description of daily life, markets, royal courts, and agriculture in 13th-century Angkor.
2. **The Inscriptions of Cambodia (*Inscriptions du Cambodge*)** — Edited and translated by George Cœdès (EFEO, 8 volumes, 1937–1966). Primary epigraphic Sanskrit and Old Khmer inscriptions recording temple endowments, royal genealogies, and corvée slave allocations.
3. **The Royal Chronicles of Ayutthaya** — Translated by Richard D. Cushman (The Siam Society, 2000). Siamese historical accounts of the 14th–15th century military campaigns against Angkor.

### 2. Primary Scientific & Archaeological Monographs
4. **Buckley, Brendan M., et al.** (2010) — *"Climate as a contributing factor in the demise of Angkor, Cambodia."* *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*, 107(15), 6748–6752. Landmark dendrochronological analysis of *Fokienia hodginsii* proving the 14th- and 15th-century megadroughts.
5. **Evans, Damian, et al.** (2013) — *"Uncovering archaeological landscapes at Angkor using lidar."* *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*, 110(31), 12595–12600. The foundational LiDAR mapping paper establishing Greater Angkor as a 1,000 km² low-density megacity.
6. **Penny, Dan, et al.** (2018) — *"The demise of Angkor: Systemic vulnerability of urban infrastructure to climate change."* *Science Advances*, 4(10), eaau4211. Detailed geoarchaeological analysis of canal incision and sedimentation breaches at Spean Thma.
7. **Fletcher, Roland, et al.** (2008) — *"The Water Management Network of Angkor, Cambodia."* *Antiquity*, 82(317), 658–670. Systems architecture and hydraulic modeling of the East and West Barays.
8. **Pottier, Christophe** (1999) — *Carte archéologique de la région d'Angkor: Zone Sud*. Doctoral dissertation, Université de Paris III. Foundational ground-survey mapping of domestic occupation mounds and water tanks.
9. **Groslier, Bernard-Philippe** (1979) — *"La cité hydraulique: la ville angkorienne, sa genèse ou sa crise."* *Bulletin de l'École française d'Extrême-Orient (BEFEO)*, 66, 161–202. The seminal thesis defining Angkor as a "hydraulic city."
