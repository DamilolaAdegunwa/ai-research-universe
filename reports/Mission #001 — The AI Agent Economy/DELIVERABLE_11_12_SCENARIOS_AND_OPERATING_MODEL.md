### 11.1 Detailed Scenario Profiles

#### Scenario 1: The Augmentation Equilibrium (Jevons Paradox Reigning)
* **Core Assumption:** Total demand for software expands faster than developer productivity increases. As creating software becomes 5x cheaper, organizations demand 10x more custom applications.
* **Impact on Workers:** Experienced engineers earn record compensation; junior hiring re-stabilizes around system-level verification and prompt/agent engineering.
* **Early Indicators:** Corporate software engineering job listings rebound; software development budgets expand as a percentage of total corporate revenue.

#### Scenario 2: Agentic Disruption (The Autonomous Tsunami)
* **Core Assumption:** Multi-agent swarms achieve multi-day autonomous reliability with sub-1% error rates. Entire functions (tier-1/2 customer support, basic software development, accounting) operate autonomously.
* **Impact on Workers:** Massive white-collar labor displacement. Unprecedented concentration of economic wealth among model providers and sovereign owners of proprietary distribution.
* **Early Indicators:** Mass layoffs in offshore IT services hubs; enterprise software contracts renegotiated to "outcome-based" pricing rather than seat-based licensing.

#### Scenario 3: Software Commoditization (The Zero-Marginal-Cost Collapse)
* **Core Assumption:** Software becomes a disposable utility. Code is ephemeral, generated on-the-fly for specific user sessions, and discarded.
* **Impact on Workers:** The engineering profession contracts by 70%. Value collapses into infrastructure hosting (compute/energy/GPUs) and physical real-world assets.
* **Early Indicators:** Open-source ecosystems flood with trillions of lines of ephemeral synthetic code; bespoke enterprise SaaS vendors face margin collapse.

#### Scenario 4: The Organizational Bottleneck (The Great Stagnation)
* **Core Assumption:** Technical capability advances, but enterprise legal liabilities, union resistance, legacy monolithic debt, and regulatory compliance (EU AI Act) stall enterprise adoption.
* **Impact on Workers:** Slow transition. High demand for engineers willing to maintain legacy C#/.NET and COBOL monoliths without AI dependencies.
* **Early Indicators:** Surging enterprise litigation over AI copyright/IP; insurance underwriters refusing to cover fully autonomous AI deployments.

#### Scenario 5: The Asymmetric Hybrid (The Baseline Reality)
* **Core Assumption:** Startups, tech-native firms, and solo operators achieve hyper-leverage, while heavily regulated enterprises (banking, defense, healthcare) adopt AI slowly with heavy human-in-the-loop safeguards.
* **Impact on Workers:** Extreme wage dispersion. High-agency engineers who master agentic orchestration earn $300k–$1M+; conventional developers in bureaucratic firms face stagnation and layoffs.

### 12.1 Day-in-the-Life Execution Architecture
1. **08:00 – Problem Formulation & Intent Architecture:** The individual does not open an IDE to write code. They write **System Invariant Specifications (SIS)** defining data models, state transitions, security boundaries, and non-negotiable business rules in markdown.
2. **09:00 – Agent Swarm Dispatch:** The individual dispatches autonomous agent tasks using Google Antigravity or Claude Code. One agent scaffolds the microservice in .NET 9; another agent authors comprehensive integration tests using Testcontainers; a third agent crawls regulatory documentation to verify compliance rules.
3. **11:00 – Adversarial Audit & Verification:** The individual reviews the agents' work. Rather than reading line-by-line syntax, they execute automated test suites, inspect edge-case failure logs, test concurrency boundaries under simulated load, and audit database transaction isolation.
4. **14:00 – Client & Stakeholder Trust Alignment:** The individual spends the afternoon on non-automatable work: direct client calls, understanding enterprise political dynamics, negotiating enterprise pricing, and signing off on legal/regulatory filings.
5. **17:00 – Telemetry & Self-Healing Pipeline Review:** Reviewing operational metrics, automated error reports, and deploying production updates via automated trunk-based CI/CD pipelines.
